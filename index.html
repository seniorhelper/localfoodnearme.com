Which type of business do people choose fastest?

Food and drink. When BrightLocal ranked industries by how likely a decision was to take five minutes or less, food and drink came first, followed by retail and home or garden services. The slowest were professional services, trades and home repairs, and beauty or personal care. As BrightLocal put it: people are hungry, they want something quick, and they want it nearby.

Where the searching happens

It starts on Google. It rarely ends there.

What device do people use to search for food near them?

A phone, overwhelmingly. 73% of local business searches start on a mobile device against 19% on a computer and 8% on a tablet. That holds across every age group measured, though it peaks at 86% among 30 to 44 year olds and falls to 57% among the over-60s, who are the most likely to use a computer.

The interesting number in that second table is the last one. Three quarters of people use more than one channel inside a search that mostly finishes in under half an hour. They are not switching channels because they are unsatisfied. They are cross-checking — seeing a name on one surface and verifying it on another. Which means the same restaurant has to hold up in more than one place within a few minutes, or it drops off the list.

Is Google still where people find restaurants?

Yes, but it is no longer the whole search. 52% of consumers start on Google Search and 9% on Google Maps, and 71% use Google at some point. But 39% start somewhere else entirely — social media, an AI tool, a review site — and 75% use more than one channel before deciding. Of people who start on Google, only 33% stop there.

The fastest-moving number in local search

AI went from 6% to 45% in twelve months.

And it is being used to shortlist, not to decide. That distinction matters more than the headline growth does.

What the numbers actually describe

A consumer asks an assistant for a recommendation. It names a few places. Then they go and check those places somewhere else before they act.

Only 18% of people using AI for local search felt ready to contact the business it recommended. The rest kept researching. Of those who began on an AI tool, 43% went to Google next and 39% went to social media, and a vanishing 2% used AI alone.

So AI is now a shortlisting layer sitting in front of the old process rather than replacing it. That is a specific and quite demanding position for a restaurant to be in: you have to be named by the assistant, and then survive the verification.

Why being named is a different job from ranking

Ranking is a position on a page. Being named by an assistant is being retrieved from a body of text and repeated. Those reward different things.

An assistant cannot see your beautiful photography or your ambience. It reads text, and it prefers text that is specific, structured, sourced and consistent with what other sources say. A restaurant whose hours differ between its own site, its Google profile and a directory is a restaurant an assistant has reason to skip.

Why we publish a range instead of a number

Every marketing site in this category quotes a precise search volume, and most of those numbers cannot be reproduced by the person reading them. Google Keyword Planner shows exact figures only to accounts with active spend. Without it, the tool returns bands.

So the honest version of "local food near me gets a lot of searches" is: between ten thousand and a hundred thousand a month in the United States, at low competition, in the twelve months to June 2026. That is a wide band and it is still a large number.

We take the same approach to everything on this site. Every figure carries a source, a sample size where one exists, and a date. If we cannot source it, we do not publish it — which is why you will not find the widely repeated claim that "near me" searches grew 900% on this page.

Next: how to actually find good food near you, or search the directory.

How do most people find food near them?

On a phone, through Google, in a few minutes. BrightLocal's 2026 research found 73% of local business searches start on a mobile device, 52% start on Google Search and a further 9% on Google Maps, and 71% of people touch Google at some point during the search. Three quarters use more than one channel before deciding.

How quickly do people decide where to eat?

Faster than for anything else. 75% of consumers decide which local business to use in under 30 minutes, and 28% decide in under five. When BrightLocal ranked industries by decision speed, food and drink came out fastest, ahead of retail and home services. Among people who were ready to act rather than browse, 32% decided in under five minutes.

How many restaurants does someone look at before choosing?

Three or fewer, in 72% of cases. Only 1% of people look at more than ten. Among people who already had a specific business in mind, 27% simply contacted the first result they found. The practical meaning is that a restaurant which is not in the first handful of results is not being compared and rejected. It is not being seen.

Is 'food near me' a real search term?

Yes, and a large one. Google Keyword Planner reports 'local food near me' at 10,000 to 100,000 average monthly searches in the United States for the July 2025 to June 2026 window, with low competition, alongside similar volumes for 'local restaurants near me' and 'nearby food'. Keyword Planner reports ranges rather than exact figures for accounts without active spend, which is why we publish a range.

Are people using AI to find restaurants now?

Increasingly, but not to make the final call. BrightLocal found 45% of consumers used an AI tool for local business recommendations in the past year, up from 6% the year before. But of the people who started their search on an AI tool, only 2% used it as their only channel: 43% went on to check Google and 39% checked social media. Only 18% felt ready to contact the business the AI recommended without verifying it first.

Why should a restaurant owner care about any of this?

Because the numbers describe a window, and the window is small. Three slots, five minutes, on a phone, mostly through Google but increasingly through several channels at once. Nothing about that window rewards a better menu, and everything about it rewards being present and easy to verify at the moment someone is deciding.
