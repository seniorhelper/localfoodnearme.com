Coverage, honestly

What the awarded tier can and cannot reach.

The awarded tier is only as national as the guides behind it, and those guides are not evenly spread across America. The MICHELIN Guide has arrived in the United States one region at a time over twenty years, and large parts of the country still have no coverage at all.

That is worth stating plainly, because a directory that quietly implies national award coverage would be misleading. If your city is not on this list, no restaurant there can hold a MICHELIN distinction — not because the food is worse, but because inspectors have never been sent.

The James Beard Foundation fills much of the gap. Its regional Best Chef categories cover all fifty states, and its national categories are open to anyone, which is how restaurants in places MICHELIN has never visited still appear in the awarded tier.

Below the awarded tier, imported coverage is genuinely national, because open map data does not care whether a guide has visited.

Method

How this thing is actually put together.

Why it loads in pieces

There are roughly seven hundred thousand restaurants in the United States. Sending that to a phone as a single file would be absurd, so the directory is split into shards by area and the search loads only the shard you ask for. Searching Denver downloads Denver.

The practical effect is that the directory can grow to national scale without the page getting slower, because the size of the whole dataset never affects the size of what any one visitor downloads.

Why imported listings have no page

Generating a separate web page for every imported record is technically easy and a genuinely bad idea. It produces hundreds of thousands of near-identical pages containing a name, an address and nothing a person could use.

Search engines treat that as exactly what it is. So imported records are searchable here but do not get pages. Pages exist where there is something to put on them: awarded restaurants, claimed listings and featured profiles.

What we will not do

Invent listings. Every entry traces to a published award, a restaurant that confirmed it, or an open dataset we name.

Publish details we cannot stand behind. Open map data is reliable for name, address and category and unreliable for hours and phone numbers. We show the first and ask the restaurant for the second.

Scrape Google or Yelp. Both prohibit using their data to build a competing directory, and doing it anyway would put the whole site at risk. The open datasets exist precisely so nobody has to.

Sell position. Covered above, and worth repeating because it is the thing that quietly ruins most directories.

What is coming

The imported tier is being expanded from the Overture Maps Foundation release, city by city. The awarded tier grows whenever a new selection is published — the MICHELIN Guide Colorado 2026 statewide edition is expected shortly and will add entries in towns that were never previously eligible.

How many restaurants are there in the United States?

Roughly 700,000, which is the number that shapes how this directory is engineered. It is why the data is sharded by area rather than loaded in one piece, and why imported records are searchable without each one generating its own page. A directory at that scale is a data problem before it is an editorial one.

How do I find restaurants near me in this directory?

Type a city, state or restaurant name into the search bar. The directory loads only the records for the area you search rather than the whole dataset, which keeps it fast on a phone. Results show the restaurant, its city and state, its category, and which tier the entry belongs to.

Is it free for a restaurant to be listed?

Yes. Claiming a listing costs nothing, has no trial and needs no card. If your restaurant is already in the directory you can confirm the details and they become correct; if it is not, tell us and we will add it. Featured listings, which include a written profile and a page of their own, are paid or included with our marketing work and are labelled as featured.

Can a restaurant pay for a better position in the results?

No. Search results are not sold and paid listings do not outrank anything. What payment buys is a featured listing: a profile, photography, a booking link and a page of its own, marked Featured wherever it appears. The awarded tier cannot be bought at all, because those entries come from the MICHELIN Guide and the James Beard Foundation.

Where does the directory data come from?

Award-verified entries come from published MICHELIN Guide and James Beard Foundation selections. Claimed and featured entries are confirmed by the restaurants themselves. Imported entries come from the Overture Maps Foundation, an open mapping dataset backed by Meta, Microsoft, Amazon and TomTom, which is licensed for commercial use with attribution and includes data from Foursquare's Open Source Places under Apache 2.0.

Why do imported listings have so little detail?

Because open map data carries the basics well and everything else poorly. Name, address, coordinates and category are reliable. Hours, menus, photographs and current phone numbers frequently are not. Rather than publish details we cannot stand behind, we show what is verifiable and invite the restaurant to claim the listing and fill in the rest for free.

Does every restaurant in the directory have its own page?

No, deliberately. Award-verified, claimed and featured restaurants get pages because there is something real to put on them. Imported records are searchable but do not get individual pages, because generating hundreds of thousands of thin pages from open data is bad for the reader and would rightly be treated as low-quality content by search engines.

How is this different from Google Maps or Yelp?

It is much smaller, and that is the point rather than an apology. Google and Yelp aim for every business. We are building a directory where the award-verified tier is genuinely selective, the claimed tier is confirmed by operators, and the whole thing sits alongside written guides for specific places. If you want exhaustive coverage of every takeaway in America, use Google. If you want to know which restaurants have been recognised by someone, this is more useful.

How often is the directory updated?

The awarded tier is updated whenever MICHELIN or the James Beard Foundation publishes a selection, which is several times a year. Claimed listings are updated whenever the restaurant tells us something changed. Imported records are refreshed when the Overture Maps Foundation publishes a new release, which is roughly monthly.

A listing is wrong. How do I fix it?

Use the form on this page and select 'Correct my existing listing'. We would rather have a small directory that is right than a large one that is wrong, so corrections take priority over new listings and there is no charge for either.

What areas does the directory cover?

The United States, expanding. Award-verified coverage follows the MICHELIN Guide's US regions and the James Beard Foundation's national and regional categories. Imported coverage follows the Overture Maps Foundation's US data. Alongside it we publish written guides for specific places, listed further down this page, including several outside the United States.
